## 1. The pinned framework — OI×price by stage (2043331777897676894, 99K views)

- Early pull-up: OI↑ + price↑ = longs market-buying through the book — lowest-risk stage to be long.
- Later stages: OI is a 4-way net (short liq/close = buy & OI↓; long close = sell & OI↓; long open = buy & OI↑; short open = sell & OI↑) — raw OI/price correlation is meaningless without decomposition.
- Dealer job during pull-up = chain-squeeze shorts → consolidation follows each leg. In consolidation: (a) OI↓+price↓ on 1m at the tail of liquidation runs = main force ALSO banking long OI (a shakeout variant); (b) engineered short-term breakdowns to bait shorts + shake weak longs.
- Pullbacks: if the main force holds floating-profit longs, they combine closing longs + adding shorts to smash price fast ("一根针"), squeezing longs while re-activating the tape.
- Meta-rule: pure technical OI/price analysis "doesn't mean much" — always sit in the dealer's seat: where would YOU hide positions, where would you deliberately show them.

## 5. Orderbook / tape mechanics (2053609317333622901)

Market order = taker = demand = drives price; limit order = maker = supply = blocks price and is where chips change hands. Price up ⇒ limit asks being eaten by market buys. Trader edge lives in TAKER flow (liq/stop/breakout market orders at high-liquidity zones). **Slow-grind breakouts at key technical levels = the trap ("one fish eaten twice"):** grind above the level → shorts market-cover + breakout longs market-buy → when both are in, one needle down takes both. Genuinely strong names break cleanly. Bottom-formation psychology on 1m (2044797525253841389): fast break of technical support → massive turnover + huge liq prints but price holds → snap AR → long low-volatility grind that feeds shorts confidence and bleeds longs patience → then launch.
